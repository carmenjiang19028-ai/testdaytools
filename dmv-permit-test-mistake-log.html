How to use a DMV mistake log

After each short practice round, save only the questions that show a repeatable weak area: wrong-way entry, one-way or lane direction, right-of-way, crossing signs, speed/advisory speed, documents, or score margin. Then practice the weakest area before taking another full round.

What to review first

If the missed item is a sign-control problem, use the road-sign page and review the visual cue that caused the miss. If it is a rule, retake state practice. If it is a documents or appointment issue, open the checklist and official source before test day.

Turn common misses into the next drill

Use the log as a routing tool. The point is to stop repeating full quizzes when one specific confusion is doing the damage.

Wrong-way entry

Review red entry-control signs

Do Not Enter tells you not to enter. Wrong Way tells you a movement has already put you against traffic. Practice choosing the correct action for each.

Same number, different speed decision

Two drivers see 35. One sees a white rectangle reading SPEED LIMIT 35. The other sees a yellow 35 MPH plaque under a curve warning. Which sign sets the posted legal limit, and when should the second driver slow down?

Check the decision

The white sign sets the posted maximum for that segment, not a speed you must reach. The yellow plaque advises a speed for the curve ahead; it does not replace the posted limit. Slow before entering the curve, and go slower than the advice if rain, traction, or visibility calls for it.

Check yourself: If the same curve has both a posted limit and a lower advisory speed, do not use the higher number as your target for the curve.

FHWA sign references: R2-1 speed limit sign and W13-1P advisory speed plaque.

Texas: a two-lane road meets a divided highway

You're on a two-lane road. There is no signal, stop sign, or yield sign where it meets a divided highway. A vehicle on the highway is close enough that entering would cut it off. Can you cross because there is no sign?

Check the decision

No. Stop and yield to the highway vehicle. The absence of a sign does not give the narrower road priority. Wait until you can enter or cross without interfering with traffic on the highway. If there is a traffic-control device, follow it instead of assuming this example applies.

Texas Transportation Code §545.151 · Texas Driver Handbook, Chapter 4

Illinois: two vehicles arrive at about the same time

You and another driver approach from different roads at about the same time. Neither road has priority and there are no traffic controls. The other driver is to your right. Who yields?

Check the decision

You yield to the driver on your right. That is the tie rule in this example. Do not treat it as a universal rule for every intersection: a through highway or traffic-control device can change the answer. Even when it is your turn, enter only when the way is clear.

Illinois Vehicle Code §11-901 · Illinois Rules of the Road, Right of Way
