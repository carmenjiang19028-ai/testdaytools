DMV road signs and meanings

31 common signs. Read the picture first, then say the driver action before checking the meaning.

Practice only the signs you missed. Scan to find the same groups online, or go to testdaytools.com/dmv-road-signs-cheat-sheet.html.

Red

Stop, yield, or a restriction.

Yellow

General warning. Read the symbol.

Orange

Temporary traffic control, such as work zones.

Yellow-green

School warnings and some pedestrian or bicycle warnings.

White

Often a regulation, such as speed or direction.

Blue

Services such as fuel or a hospital.

Use color as a clue, then check the shape, symbol, or words. Yellow-green alone does not set a school speed limit.

Regulatory traffic signs

These signs state laws, restrictions, right-of-way rules, or required directions.

Stop

Come to a complete stop, yield, then proceed when safe.

Yield

Slow and give the right of way when another road user has priority.

Do Not Enter

Do not enter that roadway, ramp, or restricted direction.

Wrong Way

You are facing traffic; correct your direction safely.

One Way

Traffic moves only in the arrow direction.

No U-turn

A U-turn is not allowed at that location.

All-Way Stop

Stop completely. ALL WAY means every approach stops; follow right-of-way rules.

No Right Turn

A right turn is not allowed from this approach.

No Turn on Red

Stopping first is not enough; wait until the signal allows the turn.

Keep Right

Stay to the right of an island, divider, or obstruction.

Speed Limit

The posted number is the legal maximum under normal conditions.

Do Not Pass

Passing another vehicle is prohibited in this zone.

Warning signs

These signs warn about conditions that may require lower speed, more space, or extra scanning.

Practice 19 questionsPractice 19 questions online
Merge

Traffic may join your lane; adjust speed and space.

Lane Ends

A lane will end and traffic must merge.

Slippery When Wet

Road traction may be reduced; avoid sudden maneuvers.

Signal Ahead

Prepare for a traffic light ahead.

Railroad Crossing Ahead

Prepare for tracks and obey crossing controls.

Roundabout

Slow and yield before entering a roundabout. Follow the arrows counterclockwise.

Curve

The road bends ahead; reduce speed before entering the curve.

Winding Road

Several curves are ahead; slow down and keep steady lane position.

Crossroad

Another road crosses ahead; scan both sides for entering traffic.

Side Road

A side road joins ahead; watch for traffic entering from that side.

T-Intersection

The road ends at another road; prepare to turn left or right.

Two-Way Traffic

The left arrow points down and the right arrow up. Keep right and expect oncoming traffic.

Stop Ahead

The arrow above a red octagon warns of a separate stop sign ahead. Slow now; stop at the actual control.

Bicycle Crossing

Watch for bicycles entering or crossing. Yellow-green can carry the same warning.

School, work-zone, and service signs

These signs flag special areas where driver attention, lower speed, or route information matters.

School Crossing

Watch for children and obey school-zone rules.

Pedestrian Crossing

Watch for people crossing and be ready to yield.

Workers (Work Zone)

The shovel warns of workers in or near the road. Slow down and watch for temporary directions.

Hospital

A hospital or medical service is nearby.

Animal Crossing

Animals may enter the road; slow and scan shoulders.

2. Make eight driving decisions

Pick an action in each situation, then compare your reasoning with the answer.

  1. At STOP, you can see an empty intersection beyond the stop line. What comes first?

    1. Roll past the line while checking again.
    2. Stop before the line, then yield and proceed when safe.
    3. Stop beyond the line where you have the best view.
    Check decision 1

    B. Stop before the line, then yield and proceed when safe. A clear view changes the gap decision, not where or whether to stop.

    Compare STOP and YIELD

  2. At YIELD, a pedestrian has started crossing your path. What should you do?

    1. Give way and stop if needed before proceeding.
    2. Keep rolling; only a STOP sign can require a full stop.
    3. Stop for a fixed count, then enter even if the path stays blocked.
    Check decision 2

    A. Give way and stop if needed before proceeding. Giving way may require a stop. Counting seconds does not clear a conflict.

    Compare STOP and YIELD

  3. A LANE ENDS warning and the markings show your lane will end. How should you prepare?

    1. Assume the warning only applies to traffic joining from a ramp.
    2. Move across right away; the warning gives your lane priority.
    3. Follow the markings and move into a safe gap.
    Check decision 3

    C. Follow the markings and move into a safe gap. Lane reduction means your lane ends, but the warning does not give you priority.

    Compare Merge and Lane Ends

  4. A yellow-green diamond shows a person walking. People are near the crosswalk. What now?

    1. Slow, scan for crossing people, and be ready to yield.
    2. Treat the color alone as a school speed limit.
    3. Stop at the warning sign itself, regardless of where the crossing is.
    Check decision 4

    A. Slow, scan for crossing people, and be ready to yield. The symbol warns of pedestrians; the color alone does not set a speed limit.

    Compare school and pedestrian crossings

  5. It is raining and you see SLIPPERY WHEN WET before a curve. What is the better response?

    1. Hold your speed until you feel the tires slip.
    2. Reduce speed before the curve and steer and brake smoothly.
    3. Wait until you are turning, then brake firmly.
    Check decision 5

    B. Reduce speed before the curve and steer and brake smoothly. Slow before the curve. Sudden braking or steering on wet pavement can reduce control.

    Practice wet-road and other warning signs

  6. You see a round railroad warning sign, but the tracks are not yet in view. What now?

    1. Look and listen for trains; prepare to stop and obey the crossing controls.
    2. Wait for the tracks to come into view before you start checking.
    3. Treat the round warning as the crossing itself.
    Check decision 6

    A. Look and listen for trains; prepare to stop and obey the crossing controls. The circle is an advance warning. Check before you reach the tracks.

    Compare the railroad warning and crossbuck

  7. After an orange work-zone warning, a flagger faces you with a STOP paddle. What now?

    1. Proceed slowly if the lane looks open.
    2. Stop briefly, then follow the previous vehicle without waiting for a signal.
    3. Stop as directed and wait for the flagger to signal you through.
    Check decision 7

    C. Stop as directed and wait for the flagger to signal you through. The orange sign warns; the flagger's paddle controls whether you may proceed.

    Compare flagger paddles in the Texas lesson

  8. A red slash crosses a right-turn arrow. The light is green. May you turn right?

    1. Yes, if you yield first.
    2. No. Follow a permitted movement and choose another route.
    3. Yes; the slash applies only while the light is red.
    Check decision 8

    B. No. Follow a permitted movement and choose another route. NO RIGHT TURN is not NO TURN ON RED. Green does not cancel the posted prohibition.

    Compare the signs in the Florida right-turn cases

3. Apply the rule to a different situation

Now your lane continues, but a ramp joins from the right. What should you watch for, and why does a MERGE warning not require you to leave your lane?

Check your reasoning

Watch for joining traffic and leave space. The warning does not say that your lane ends; use the actual lane markings and traffic conditions before changing lanes.

Understand the distinctions behind a missed sign

  • Stop vs. Yield: an empty road does not make the signs interchangeable
  • Do Not Enter vs. Wrong Way: prevent the entry, then recognize a direction error
  • Merge vs. Lane Ends: joining traffic is not the same as losing a lane
