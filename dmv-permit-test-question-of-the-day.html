Answer: Come to a complete stop and yield before moving
Stop completely even if the road looks empty. If there is a marked stop line, stop before it rather than rolling into the crosswalk to see past an obstruction. Then yield and check that the path is clear before proceeding.

Florida School buses

A Florida school bus is stopped with its stop arm out across a paved center turn lane. You are approaching from the opposite direction. What must you do?

A paved turn lane does not qualify. Florida's opposite-direction exception requires at least 5 feet of unpaved separation, a raised median, or a physical barrier. Neither going slowly nor looking for children replaces the stop on this road.

Florida Signs and signals

At a Florida intersection, the signal facing you is dark but the signals controlling cross traffic still work. There is a marked stop line and no officer is directing traffic. What should you do?

You still have to stop when only your signal is out. If there is no stop line, stop before the crosswalk, or before entering the intersection at a point with a view of approaching traffic if there is no crosswalk. Do not assume drivers facing working signals will stop.

Florida Sharing the road

You are behind a bicyclist in your Florida travel lane. Oncoming traffic leaves room to pass with only about two feet of clearance. What should you do?

Florida requires a safe gap of at least three feet when overtaking someone on a bicycle in the same travel lane. If you cannot leave that gap safely, stay behind; neither a lower speed nor a horn makes two feet enough.

Pennsylvania Distracted driving

You are stopped at a red light in Pennsylvania. A friend sends a message, and no emergency is involved. When can you pick up your phone to read it?

Under Paul Miller's Law, a temporary stop at a light or in traffic still counts as driving. Shifting to park in the lane does not change that. Move out of traffic to a safe place where the car can remain stopped before handling the phone.

Pennsylvania Sharing the road

You are behind a bicyclist on a two-lane Pennsylvania road. You have a long clear view and no oncoming traffic. Leaving four feet would require crossing the double yellow line. What is permitted?

Pennsylvania permits crossing the center line when needed to pass a bicycle safely with at least four feet of space. Recheck the sightline and oncoming traffic before moving left. If you cannot make the pass with that clearance at a safe speed, stay behind.

Pennsylvania School buses

In Pennsylvania in November 2026, you approach a stopped school bus from behind. Its red lights are flashing and its stop arm is extended. What is the minimum required stopping distance before reaching the bus?

For the date in this question, Pennsylvania requires at least 10 feet. Act 89 raises the minimum to 15 feet on October 22, 2027. You may leave more room now; the question asks for the legal minimum. Stay stopped until the lights stop flashing, the arm retracts, and the children reach safety.

New Jersey Sharing the road

On a narrow New Jersey road posted at 20 mph, you cannot legally change lanes or give the bicyclist ahead four feet of space. It is not safe to pass. What is the correct response?

When a lane change and four feet of space are unavailable, the rule calls for 25 mph or the lower posted limit and readiness to stop. Here the lower limit is 20 mph. The speed reduction is not permission to pass when it would endanger anyone.

New Jersey School buses

On a New Jersey road posted at 45 mph, a stopped school bus is unloading children across a raised median. You are traveling in the opposite direction. What is the highest speed allowed while passing on your side?

The raised median and your opposite direction matter: New Jersey limits you to 10 mph until you are past the bus. On the same side, or if only painted lines separate opposing traffic on a road like this, the usual rule is to stop at least 25 feet away.

New Jersey Move Over

On a New Jersey highway, a disabled car on the shoulder has its hazard lights on. Traffic in the left lane prevents you from safely moving over. What should you do?

New Jersey's rule applies to stationary vehicles displaying flashing or blinking lights, including this disabled car. When moving over is unsafe, slow below the limit to a speed appropriate for conditions and be ready to stop. New Jersey does not set one fixed reduction. Follow an officer's directions if given.

New Jersey Move Over law: P.L. 2025, chapter 248
