Arrival, closing time, and testing start are different

College Board's sample SAT Weekend schedule has regular check-in at 7:45 a.m. and doors closing at 8:00 a.m. Your ticket may give another time; plan from its arrival instruction, not the later closing time. For an approved loaner, use the earlier time you were given; do not subtract another 30 minutes if it already includes early pickup.

Finish marked questions before leaving that module

Suppose a Reading and Writing question is taking too long and Bluebook shows six minutes left. Make your best choice, mark it, and review other unanswered items in that module. Return if time remains. The next module has its own clock: once you move forward, unused time and marked questions do not carry over.

Plan pickup separately

The standard timed sections and break total 144 minutes. Desk check-in, actual start time, answer submission, and staff dismissal can make your visit longer. For accommodations or School Day, use your coordinator's instructions, not a standard end-time estimate. After the test, note general pacing problems, such as rushed final minutes; do not reproduce real exam questions.

FAQ

What time should I arrive for the SAT?

Follow the confirmed arrival instruction from your ticket, approved loaner notice, or school. Plan to reach the check-in area earlier than that time, not just the parking lot.

Can I return to a question after I move to the next SAT module?

No. Bluebook lets you revisit questions while time remains within the current module, but not after you move on.

If my loaner instructions say to arrive at 7:15, do I subtract another 30 minutes?

No. Enter the confirmed 7:15 pickup time as given, then subtract your travel, walking, and buffer once.

Does 2 hours 24 minutes tell me when to arrange pickup?

No. That is standard testing time plus the normal break, not time from arrival to dismissal. Ask the site or school about pickup, particularly if you have accommodations.
