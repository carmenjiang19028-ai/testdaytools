Rules to know in New York

  • Right on red in New York City Wait for green unless a sign allows the turn. Outside New York City, the default rule allows a right turn after stopping and yielding unless a sign prohibits it. (Chapter 4: Traffic Control)
  • School bus on a divided highway Drivers on both sides must stop for a school bus with its red lights flashing, even on a divided highway. Stop at least 20 feet from the bus. (Chapter 6: Passing, School Buses)
  • Permit supervision A learner needs a licensed supervising driver at least 21 years old, qualified for the vehicle. Junior permit rules also vary by part of the state. (Chapter 1: Driver Licenses)
  • Passing cyclists and using the shoulder Pass a bicyclist on the left at a safe distance. Do not use the shoulder as a passing lane unless directed by a traffic officer or allowed by a sign. (Chapter 6: Passing)

Junior permit driving is not allowed there from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. (Chapter 1: Driver Licenses, Regional Restrictions for a Junior Permit)

Practice New York rules

Say what you would do before opening the answer. If you miss a scenario, review the named section in the handbook before trying the second example.

1. You're in Queens at a steady red with no turn-on-red sign. The crosswalk and traffic are clear. Can you turn right?

Answer: Wait for green.

Wait for green unless a sign allows the turn. Outside New York City, the default rule allows a right turn after stopping and yielding unless a sign prohibits it.

Manual: Chapter 4: Traffic Control

2. You're outside New York City, stopped at a steady red without a turn restriction. Can you turn now if you have yielded and it is clear?

Answer: Yes, if it is safe.

Wait for green unless a sign allows the turn. Outside New York City, the default rule allows a right turn after stopping and yielding unless a sign prohibits it.

Manual: Chapter 4: Traffic Control

3. An approaching school bus stops with red lights flashing across a grassy median. Can you keep going?

Answer: No. Stop.

Drivers on both sides must stop for a school bus with its red lights flashing, even on a divided highway. Stop at least 20 feet from the bus.

Manual: Chapter 6: Passing, School Buses

4. You follow a school bus that stops with its red lights on. Is stopping one car length away always enough?

Answer: No. Leave at least 20 feet.

Drivers on both sides must stop for a school bus with its red lights flashing, even on a divided highway. Stop at least 20 feet from the bus.

Manual: Chapter 6: Passing, School Buses

5. Your 20-year-old sibling has a full license. Can they be your only supervisor?

Answer: No. They are too young to supervise.

A learner needs a licensed supervising driver at least 21 years old, qualified for the vehicle. Junior permit rules also vary by part of the state.

Manual: Chapter 1: Driver Licenses

6. A 25-year-old has only a motorcycle license. Can they supervise your car practice?

Answer: No. They need a license valid for the vehicle.

A learner needs a licensed supervising driver at least 21 years old, qualified for the vehicle. Junior permit rules also vary by part of the state.

Manual: Chapter 1: Driver Licenses

7. There is no clear gap to the left of a cyclist, but the paved shoulder is empty. Can you pass on the shoulder?

Answer: No. Wait for a safe pass on the left.

Pass a bicyclist on the left at a safe distance. Do not use the shoulder as a passing lane unless directed by a traffic officer or allowed by a sign.

Manual: Chapter 6: Passing

8. You've moved left around a cyclist, but your rear bumper is still next to the bicycle. Can you move right?

Answer: No. Clear the cyclist before returning.

Pass a bicyclist on the left at a safe distance. Do not use the shoulder as a passing lane unless directed by a traffic officer or allowed by a sign.

Manual: Chapter 6: Passing

9. A qualifying instructor meets you in Brooklyn at noon, but the car has no instructor's brake. Can you practice in it?

Answer: No. The vehicle must have dual controls.

In the five boroughs, junior permit practice requires dual brakes and a qualifying supervisor such as a licensed parent or instructor. Junior permit driving is not allowed there from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m.

Manual: Chapter 1: Driver Licenses, Regional Restrictions for a Junior Permit

10. Your licensed parent has arranged a dual-control car at 9:30 p.m. in Manhattan. Is it legal for you to practice with a junior permit?

Answer: No. The city nighttime restriction still applies.

In the five boroughs, junior permit practice requires dual brakes and a qualifying supervisor such as a licensed parent or instructor. Junior permit driving is not allowed there from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m.

Manual: Chapter 1: Driver Licenses, Regional Restrictions for a Junior Permit

Can you turn right on red in New York?

Same round red light, no turn signs, no green arrow: one intersection is in Albany, the other is in Queens. Can a passenger car turn right after stopping at both?

Not at both. In Albany, the car may turn right after a full stop and yielding to people and traffic, if no signal, sign, or marking forbids it. Queens is in New York City. With no sign allowing a right turn on red, the driver waits for a signal that permits the turn.

Now the Queens intersection posts a sign allowing a right turn on red, but someone is still crossing. Can you turn now?

No. Stop completely and yield. The sign allows a turn only when it is otherwise legal and safe; it does not give you priority over someone in the crosswalk.

Back in Albany, the light shows a steady red right arrow instead of a round red light. No one is coming. Does stopping make the right turn legal?

No. Do not turn in the direction of a steady red arrow. Wait for a signal allowing that turn; the usual rule for a round red light does not apply.
