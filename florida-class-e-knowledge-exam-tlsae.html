Official context

Florida Class E steps in plain order

Use FLHSMV pages for final rules. This page turns the official pieces into a practical study and appointment map.

TLSAE or DETSCourse step

Under 18 and applying for the first time: start with DETS. Age 18 or older: start with TLSAE unless an exception applies. Completion does not pass the Class E Knowledge Exam.

Class E Knowledge Exam50-question knowledge test

FLHSMV describes the exam as 50 multiple-choice questions covering traffic laws, safe driving practices, and identifying traffic controls.

Passing rule40 of 50 correct

FLHSMV lists 40 correct answers, or 80 percent, as the passing score.

Permit issuanceDocument and office step

A passed exam result still needs to fit the applicant's document, age, consent, fee, provider, and service-center path.

Online exam recordProvider submission

For online Class E testing, FLHSMV says test results are submitted automatically, but the Parent Proctoring Form and applicant documents still matter.

Third-party testingPossible random re-test

FLHSMV warns that a pass through an authorized third-party administrator can still be followed by a mandatory random re-test before issuance.

Read the words that change a Florida test answer

These are original Florida study examples, not official questions. For each one, name the usual rule, then identify the word or condition that changes what you should do.

Unless: a posted sign removes the permission

You stop at the line for a steady circular red. The intersection is clear, but you see a NO TURN ON RED sign. Can you turn right now?

Show reasoning
  1. Start with the red light: stop at the marked line. A right turn can be allowed after stopping and yielding.
  2. Read the exception: unless a sign prohibits it. This sign does. Wait until the signal allows the turn and the way is clear.

Seeing no traffic does not cancel the sign. Unless tells you which condition blocks the usual permission.

Now there is no sign, but a person is using the crosswalk. Can you turn after stopping?

Check

Not yet. The sign exception no longer applies, but you still have to yield. Turn only when you can do so safely and legally.

Florida Statutes 316.075(1)(c): turning at a steady red

Not less than: the number is a minimum

You are behind a cyclist in your travel lane. There would be three feet of clearance, but you cannot see far enough around a bend to pass safely. Should you pass now?

Show reasoning
  1. Not less than three feet means three feet or more, not three feet or less.
  2. That distance is a minimum. If you cannot make the pass safely, stay a safe distance behind the cyclist.

Meeting the distance alone is not permission to pass. The move itself must be safe.

The road straightens, but you could leave only two and a half feet. What do you do?

Check

Stay back. Better visibility does not remove the minimum clearance.

Florida Statutes 316.083(2): passing a cyclist in the same lane

Until: look for the event that ends the wait

You are stopped behind a school bus. A child reaches the sidewalk, but the bus still displays its stop signal. Can you pass?

Show reasoning
  1. Until tells you what ends the restriction. For a driver behind the bus, wait until its stop signal is withdrawn.
  2. Keep looking for children and other traffic before moving. One child reaching the sidewalk does not end the stop.

The first thing you can see happening is not necessarily the event the rule names.

Instead, you approach in the opposite direction on a divided road, with a raised median between you and the bus. Does Florida require you to stop for that bus?

Check

No, not under the school-bus rule in this situation. Florida's exception applies to opposing traffic separated by a raised median, a physical barrier, or at least five feet of unpaved space. It does not excuse a driver following the bus.

Florida Statutes 316.172: stopping for a school bus

Keep three records separate

An approved course completion answers the education requirement. The Class E Knowledge Exam is a separate result. Your documents and office visit determine whether the learner's license can be issued.

Prepare for the English-language knowledge exam

Florida's published testing policy requires English for driver license exams, including the Class E Knowledge Exam, under the statewide update effective February 6, 2026. A translated study guide is not a translated exam. While practicing, pause on words that change the required action: prohibited means not allowed; yield means give the right of way; except signals a case that does not follow the general rule. If you need to discuss testing arrangements, confirm them with your test provider before booking.

Which Florida driver education course should you take?

Check your age and licensing history before paying for a course. A practice score or test result does not determine which course you need.

Under 18, first license

Start with the 6-hour DETS course

For a first Florida learner's license when you have never been licensed elsewhere, use an approved Driver Education Traffic Safety (DETS) course. Check whether your school already offers a qualifying program. A new 4-hour TLSAE course is not a substitute for this path.

Florida Department of Education: DETS
Age 18 or older, first license

Start with the 4-hour TLSAE course

Adult first-time applicants ordinarily need Traffic Law and Substance Abuse Education (TLSAE). Under Florida law, prior licensing in another jurisdiction or a qualifying Florida Department of Education driver education course can exempt an adult from TLSAE.

Pinellas Tax Collector: adult course and provider list
Prior license or course

Verify your record before enrolling again

Ask the issuing office whether your current permit, out-of-state license, or completed school course meets your requirement. The transition for minors who completed TLSAE before August 1, 2025 was limited to one year from course completion; do not rely on that certificate to qualify now.

FAQ

Is TLSAE or DETS the same as the Florida Class E Knowledge Exam?

No. TLSAE or DETS is a course requirement in many first-time driver paths. The Class E Knowledge Exam is the separate knowledge test on traffic laws, safe driving practices, and traffic controls.

How many questions are on the Florida Class E Knowledge Exam?

FLHSMV says the Class E Knowledge Exam has 50 multiple-choice questions, and 40 correct answers, or 80 percent, are needed to pass.

If I pass the Florida Class E Knowledge Exam online, am I done?

Do not assume the whole learner-permit process is finished. Confirm the document review, fee, age or consent, photo or issuance step, provider rules, and service-center process for your path.

Do I need a paper certificate after passing the Florida Class E Knowledge Exam online?

FLHSMV says online Class E test results are submitted automatically. Before visiting an office, confirm the provider instructions, Parent Proctoring Form, identity documents, Social Security number, residential address, and local issuance process.

Can Florida make me retake a Class E Knowledge Exam after a third-party pass?

Yes. FLHSMV warns that customers who pass through an authorized third-party administrator may be selected for a mandatory random re-test without prior notice before issuance.

What should I study first for the Florida Class E exam?

Start with traffic controls, road signs, right of way, traffic laws, and safe driving practices. Use missed answers to choose the next focused drill instead of retaking random mixed tests.
